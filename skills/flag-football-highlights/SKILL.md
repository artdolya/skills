# Flag Football Highlights

Create a memorable family keepsake for parents and grandparents: achievements, effort, teamwork, personalities, tension, humor, and shared emotions. Tell the actual game story, including consequential setbacks. A coach-review cut is an explicitly requested variant. Current user instructions take precedence.

There is **no target runtime, episode ceiling, “best N” selection, equal-player quota, or requirement to fit one music track**. Let meaningful material determine duration. Trim dead time and redundant setup while preserving complete action, context, and reactions; never discard a meaningful unique moment just to shorten the video.

For a new cut: **retrieve game/team facts → inventory all sources → review and index the whole collection → reconcile identities/events/chronology → consolidate factual questions → compose → verify coverage and delivery**. For a narrow revision, apply the relevant steps to affected events and dependencies, preserve prior accepted work, and do not imply a new full-collection audit was performed.

## Availability on each device

This package provides the editing workflow and reference material. Footage, music, team/game records, earlier chats, and editable ChatCut projects remain separate resources; installing the skill does not transfer them or grant access. Use the requested ChatCut surface and its relevant installed skills/tools. Resolve the football workspace on this device using the [team-memory guide](references/team-memory.md) before reading or updating game records. Report missing access precisely and continue work supported by available evidence.

## Review the whole collection before selection

- Read the [review contract](references/review-contract.md) when creating or updating the source manifest and event ledger. Inventory every supplied source with stable ID, filename, duration, game assignment, review status, reviewed/unreviewed intervals, access/quality limitations, and linked events. Keep unresolved game assignments explicit.
- Review each source from beginning to end at sufficient temporal and visual resolution to detect plays and meaningful reactions, including material after apparent play completion. Contact sheets, sparse samples, filenames, transcripts, and automated highlight suggestions help navigation; they do not establish complete review. Record the inspection actually performed. If the available tools cannot inspect an interval adequately, mark it unreviewed rather than claiming coverage.
- Index **every distinct play and meaningful non-play episode before selection or trimming**. One file may contain several events; one event may span several files. Keep stable source intervals independent of timeline placement. Native IDs/placements remain null until implemented and for excluded events; missing timeline IDs must not prevent indexing.
- Do not stop reviewing because enough highlights have been found. Inaccessible or insufficiently inspected footage remains a precise coverage gap and prevents a complete-collection claim. Continue useful work on reviewed material and clearly label any partial assembly.
- Link alternate angles and overlapping recordings to one canonical event while retaining their unique action, context, and reactions. Exact duplicate sources may reuse a verified canonical review with evidence of duplication; report them separately from directly inspected sources. Similar uniforms, formations, or filenames do not prove two plays are duplicates.
- Keep games, teams, periods, scores, and outcomes separate. Establish chronology from evidence; do not invent game order or halftime boundaries from filenames or edited runtime alone.

## Retrieve and verify player identities

- For a known team, read its shared identity record, current game roster, and relevant original human corrections before asking for names or writing named banners. Use the [team-memory guide](references/team-memory.md) to locate records for the current team and season. Retrieve original corrections from accessible earlier chats when records are missing, contradictory, or incomplete; assistant summaries are not human confirmation. This reusable skill contains no roster; names and team/season mappings come from the current workspace evidence.
- Reuse confirmed names, aliases, number history, and uniform exceptions as evidence to check against the current game. Verify team, current jerseys, participation, and any reassignment evidence. Keep attendance, actions, outcomes, scores, timestamps, and spending approvals game-specific. Do not reset every identity because one player is unresolved.
- Track identity as **confirmed**, **tentative**, or **unresolved**, separately from action/outcome confidence. Publish a name only for an explicit human identification anchored to the exact event, or an unambiguous current-footage match to a confirmed team/season mapping. Historical jersey numbers alone do not confirm the current actor.
- For visual identification, trace the same player from identifying evidence through the credited action. Check crossings, occlusion, camera movement, handoffs, and uniform exceptions. A nearby player's readable number or an assumed track through an occlusion cannot identify the actor. Preserve conflicting evidence and the precise unresolved question.
- An unknown identity never justifies dropping an important episode. Retain it with a specific factual action-only banner where supported; keep the attribution gap visible in review records. Do not invent a name, shared credit, or action to make the record look complete.
- Save newly confirmed facts in the workspace team record and current game's roster/ledger, with original source and game scope. Preserve qualifications and superseded facts as history. Ordinary video corrections update those records, not this installed skill.

## Include meaningful moments and audit actual actions

- Use this non-exhaustive discovery checklist: touchdowns and conversions; turnovers; catches, runs, handoffs, flag pulls, stops, pass breakups, pursuit, containment, and support; consequential attempts, setbacks, recoveries, and turning points; individual contributions without a score; encouragement, celebrations, reactions, humor, huddles, and family memories. Include opponent moments needed to understand the game and every user-requested moment located in the footage.
- Include all located key moments and meaningful unique contributions by default. Favor inclusion when editorial value is uncertain. Do not require the user to nominate moments independent review should discover. Repeated successful actions may be distinct meaningful moments; selective replays do not restrict inclusion of the original live plays.
- Give every indexed event a disposition: **included**, **excluded with a concrete reason**, or **unresolved with the exact issue**. Included is a decision, not proof of placement. Runtime, soundtrack length, a filled quota, similarity to other plays, or an unknown name are insufficient exclusion reasons for a meaningful unique event. Preserve explicit user deletions; retain source anchors and reasons for excluded material.
- Inspect each action through its finish, using closer sequential footage or adjacent source frames around release, catch/control, flag detachment, and scoring. Record lead/support roles, action, outcome, separate evidence/status, banner, and replay decision. A matched human correction supersedes a visual guess; record genuine conflicts rather than silently reversing it.
- Distinguish flag pulls, shared pulls, containment, cover support, interception attempts, completed interceptions, catches, incompletions, touchdowns, and conversions. Proximity does not prove a pull. A raised glove does not prove a catch; continued possession and the preceding action distinguish a reception from a keeper.
- Reconcile the recap and known scoring with located footage, while independently discovering moments absent from the recap. Credit scorer and passer separately when supported. A matching final score does not prove complete discovery. Investigate missing expected clips; report “not located in the reviewed footage” rather than “never recorded” unless established. Do not invent scores or use an unsupported running score.

## Resolve remaining questions together

- Retrieve accessible context and inspect ambiguous footage closely before asking the user. After the collection review, consolidate remaining questions where practical: missing game metadata, identities, outcomes, and halftime facts. For each event supply a stable event ID, exact source interval, useful frame/clip reference when available, established facts, and the precise missing fact. Do not ask the user to rewatch the collection or repeat accessible answers.
- An access problem may need an earlier question, and newly contradictory evidence may justify a later focused question. Aiming for fewer rounds must never pressure the editor to guess. Continue resolved work while answers are pending; keep placeholders draft-only and do not finalize uncertain factual cards.
- Reuse current preferences and approvals within scope. These evidence checks do not introduce additional approval stops for authorized ordinary edits. Preserve the credit-consuming-operation rule below.

## Compose the family story and deliver the package

For a new cut, deliver all applicable elements below; a narrow revision preserves accepted elements and changes only its authorized scope.

| Element | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| YouTube cover | Deliver a separate thumbnail from real game/team imagery; verify visible participants and factual text, and readability at thumbnail size. Match the video typography/colors. |
| Editable ChatCut video | Open the correct project in the requested ChatCut surface, verify media availability and native timeline state, and provide its project reference/link where supported. Creation or export alone does not prove the project is open. Report access/opening failures precisely. |
| Opening before game footage | Show the supplied date, teams, location, and tournament/stage information, with **no score or first-half recap**. Add a short user-grounded teaser that creates intrigue without inventing circumstances or prematurely revealing the ending. Reserve the account of how the first half unfolded for halftime. Resolve missing necessary facts together; omit unavailable optional details. |
| Halftime summary | At an established halftime boundary, include a brief, readable summary (usually 1–2 short sentences) of how the first half unfolded and the situation at the break, using supplied or verified facts. Show the supplied or verified halftime score when available. A score alone does not satisfy the summary when narrative context is known. Keep the perspective at halftime: do not reveal second-half events or the final result. If the score is unknown, omit the numbers and use supported narrative facts; if narrative facts are unavailable, show only known information and retain the precise gap. If the boundary is unknown, keep the card pending and resolve its location; never substitute the edit's midpoint. |
| Moment banners | Use specific verified actions/contributions; publish names only at the identity threshold above. Keep text readable and clear of the action. |
| Ending | Give a concise confirmed final result. A known win/loss can be stated without a numeric score; include numbers only when known. Do not invent a score, tournament advancement, or award. An unknown result remains a disclosed factual gap. |
| Music | Match the emotional arc to the result and user framing, cover the complete edit, preserve useful field sound, and respect established vocal/instrumental choices and spending scope. |

An opening teaser can stay spoiler-free: “Every snap. Every effort. Until the final whistle.” Reserve a factual recap of missed chances, setbacks, or the halftime deficit for the halftime summary, using only facts established for that game.

- For a confirmed named key play, lead with **name + concrete action/outcome**. A banner template is “[PLAYER NAME] PULLS THE FLAG!” with a supported second line, “[TEAMMATE NAME] READY TO BACK THEM UP.” Substitute verified names before publication; never display template placeholders. Readiness/coverage must not become shared pull credit. When the name is unresolved but the action is clear, “FLAG PULL!” is a truthful fallback; it does not resolve attribution. Vague “team effort” captions must not replace a known concrete action or hide unresolved work.
- Retain meaningful incomplete passes, attempts, encouragement, humorous moments, and reactions without requiring a coach request. Describe supported effort without promoting it to a completion. Give participants meaningful representation when footage supports it, without inventing achievements or equal counts. Keep humor affectionate, label invented quips as editorial captions, and do not mock mistakes or celebrate playing through pain.
- Trim idle postgame setup and walk-off, but preserve meaningful huddles, celebrations, and emotional reactions. Do not impose a short postgame cap. A question about an MVP or award is not approval to add it; research requested psychological advice separately.

## Replays and sound

- Default to a short labeled **slow-motion replay** for each confirmed team touchdown and interception, plus representative clear, meaningful flag pulls. Avoid replaying every routine stop. Each eligible event needs a decision: **included**, **omitted with a specific reason**, or **unresolved with the remaining question**; record whether playback is actually slowed. Respect explicit replay choices. A cut with eligible events cannot silently finish with zero replays or only normal-speed duplicates.
- A newly confirmed catch, pull, score, or actor correction reopens that event's banner and replay decision. Do the authorized native work; a chosen replay must not remain merely a ledger suggestion.
- Keep the normal-speed play and enough replay setup/finish to understand it. Select speed/crop from source frame rate/resolution, preserve contributors, and inspect motion at delivery size. For 60 fps delivered at 30 fps, 0.5x is a useful starting point; for 30 fps, evaluate a brief modest slowdown around 0.75–0.8x. Source 30 fps alone is not a reason to skip all slowdowns. If output is unreadable or unacceptably juddery, substitute normal speed or omit with the specific limitation recorded; do not call it slow motion. Do not promise interpolation/upscaling recovers missing detail. Verify native rate, label, framing, and muted/ducked slowed field audio.
- Choose tension followed by celebration for a comeback victory, determination/warmth/pride for a loss, and an appropriate neutral treatment if the result is unknown. Use the actual user framing; do not force triumph onto every game. Carry forward vocal/instrumental choices across replacements. A previously approved song may serve as a style reference; it does not automatically authorize selection, generation, or purchase for another game.
- Arrange music after episode timing is settled. Fit music to the complete story, never the story to one track. Check full coverage, repeats/crossfades, ending, and intelligible field sound. Claim listening or motion-playback review only when actually performed; stills and waveforms prove narrower facts.

## Propagate corrections without losing timestamp references

1. Snapshot the current native timeline. Resolve every user timestamp to that version, source interval, and native item before moving anything.
2. Apply timing-neutral corrections first; work from the end backward when changes shift time. Check requested filenames/ranges against existing clips before adding anything.
3. For an identity correction, inspect affected same-game appearances and update verified matches: names, roles, confidence/status, outcomes, evidence, banners, replay labels/decisions, cover/credits when affected, and review map. Retire stale “name unresolved” conclusions while preserving historical snapshots and genuine remaining action uncertainty.
4. Resolve pending timestamp-based corrections before replay insertions or other duration changes. Then implement authorized timing changes and regenerate affected overlays, music, cards, credits, and old-to-new map. Preserve stable source anchors.
5. Re-read native state and inspect composed output at corrected action and entry/exit boundaries. Preserve accepted corrections and user deletions during later music/banner/cover-only work. A stale preview calls for fresh state, not a duplicate fix.

## Verify coverage, attribution, and delivery separately

Before calling a new cut ready, or a revision complete within its affected scope:

- **Source → timeline:** reconcile the manifest and every indexed event with actual native state. Every key/included event must have verified placement containing the required action, finish, and context, or a justified disclosed exclusion. Setup-only placement does not satisfy coverage. Review exclusions again for overlooked family/story value. Identify every unreviewed interval, missing expected event, and unresolved potentially important episode.
- **Timeline → evidence:** trace each name, action, score, card, and replay attribution to the correct source interval or matched human confirmation. Check lead/support roles, current identity mappings, and scoring credits; no stale unknowns, unsupported names, or evidence borrowed from another play.
- Verify replay decisions are implemented or have explicit exclusions; normal-speed duplication does not satisfy a slow-motion decision. Inspect composed labels, trims, framing, entry/exit boundaries, opening/ending, and music/overlay coverage through the revised duration. Verify the separate cover and actual ChatCut handoff.
- Report a compact summary: sources directly reviewed/total, verified duplicates separately, unreviewed intervals, included/excluded/unresolved event counts, key-event dispositions, missing expected footage, and remaining attribution/metadata gaps. Counts alone are not proof; source and native anchors substantiate them.
- State **coverage completeness**, **attribution completeness**, and **delivery verification** independently. An unnamed but factually described key moment may be covered while attribution remains incomplete. A successful render or matching score cannot make the package fully verified. Disclose precise remaining work without hiding it in generic captions; these checks are not another user-approval gate.

When creating or changing this skill, use the [behavioral evaluation cases](references/evaluation-cases.md), including fresh-agent forward tests given only the skill and raw inputs, without expected answers. Distinguish text-based workflow tests from actual footage-based identification tests; report only validation actually performed.

## Tools, spending, and documentary covers

- Use the relevant installed ChatCut skills for the active surface; read hosted plugin basics before hosted tools. Keep the native timeline editable. If the requested surface is unavailable, report it rather than switching products or flattening the edit.
- Get agreement on episode list, attribution, banners, and replay placement before additional credit-consuming ChatCut operations, including soundtrack generation and paid rendering/export, unless current instructions explicitly authorize that operation before review. Consolidate required spending approval around the concrete plan and reuse approval within scope. Proceed with authorized ordinary native edits, imports, and corrections without another confirmation. One generated track's approval does not authorize extra variants or purchases.
- Use real game/team images for documentary covers and verify visible participants. Never add an absent teammate or label an unverified partial group as the full roster. Check faces, jersey numbers, people count, text, date, and any score after generation; describe a generated interpretation accurately.
- Artwork creation, timeline insertion, export, and publication are separate actions; a cover request alone does not authorize publication.
